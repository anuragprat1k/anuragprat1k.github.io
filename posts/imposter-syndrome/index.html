Imposter Syndrome

April 8, 2026

The fall of 2013 was the best semester of my life. The main event of undergraduate degrees in India, job placements were ahead of us. Conventional wisdom was to prepare interview style questions. But I took a slightly unconventional path. I spent nights doing competitive programming, and still proudly lookback at the SPOJ Hall of Fame. When placements came around, I felt strong and had a feeling that great things were waiting for me. And they were - I exceeded everyone’s expectations and got placed in Microsoft on Day 0, coming close even to Redmond. It was truly the first time, and maybe the only, when I did something just for the love of it and it fed my soul and body like nothing else.

A year into software engineering however, I knew I needed something more applied. I contemplated going to grad school but couldn’t get in because my GPA wasn’t great so I moved to Redmond instead. The calling to do something more kept tugging, I was doing online coursework, and just pure intention landed me in MIRA, where I was completely out of my depth. I had good intentions but not the passion for it. In many ways I was treating it as a way to get my foot in the door at Meta - which it did. So I moved on, successful in a way but also more deeply failing to do it for the love of it.

At Meta, I still wasn’t in love. I was starting to use ML as a bargaining chip, more than falling in love with it. I was learning for sure and was surrounded by exceptional people. I tried to do something complicated again and failed - I didn’t want to do the cheap easy tasks. I met H, who was someone who really did his work like he cared for it and less like it was a leverage. I envied him.

I moved to FAIR, the final bargain in some sense, of wanting to move to my favorite city in the world. Since being rejected from grad school, I had philandeerd - committed a series of loose and fast moves, trying to get somewhere the wrong way. I wish now that someone had told me after my grad school rejections that I just needed to keep doing things for love and not use them as leverage to get somewhere.

At FAIR, I struggled again, because I was doing it to meet performance goals, not for love. I hated them for holding me to random performance goals but never saw that love was the missing piece.

At Amazon, the pattern repeated. The AS conversion became the next target, the system helped externalize the blame - leaders were bad, not me. I moved from Titan, to Agents, to Nova. In between all this there were some sparks - the system 2 idea that stemmed from Robert’s project and was the best idea in the Bedrock Innovation Program. My scientific acumen was recognized eventually and I finally turned AS. I came to Nova and still did the work, but lack of love meant that I got consumed by the task in front of me again.

The absence of love doesn’t mean I didn’t work, just that I didn’t work on the right things. The absence of love meant that I also externalized blame. So no wonder that I have only had 1 promo since 2017!

And so when I approach interviews today, I approach it with dread still. Because in trying to keep up with pretenses, I haven’t done the things I would do in love with research. So I am afraid of being found out. So I cram and worry. Every move since has been optimizing for positioning rather than intrinsic drive.

  • 2013 was the peak — competitive programming done purely for love led to your best outcome (Microsoft Day 0).
  • Everything after was positioning — grad school rejection triggered a series of moves (MIRA → Meta → FAIR → Amazon) where research was used as leverage rather than pursued for its own sake.
  • The topics were right, the relationship was wrong — you picked genuinely interesting areas (pure research, agentic RL), but engaged with them instrumentally rather than surrendering to the work itself. That prevented going deep enough or taking the right risks.
  • Split attention as symptom — even at Amazon, you interviewed elsewhere every year, failing each time. The constant exit-seeking is itself a form of not surrendering — hard to go deep when part of you is always looking out.
  • The pattern: instrumental relationship to work → underperformance → externalizing blame → repeat. Only 1 promo since 2017 as a result.
  • Interview dread stems from fear of being exposed — the instrumental relationship to work left gaps that cramming can’t fill.
  • The insight: return to 2013 mode — surrender to the work, let outcomes follow.
