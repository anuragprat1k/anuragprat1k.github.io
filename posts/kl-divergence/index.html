KL divergence

A conceptual and mathematical deep dive into Kullback-Leibler divergence — intuition, derivation, and practical insights.
April 8, 2025

I found out recently, much to my embarassment, that my understanding of KL divergence was superficial at best. This blog summarizes the essentials

KL divergence is one of those deceptively simple concepts that shows up everywhere — from variational inference to language modeling to GANs — yet it’s often glossed over.

In this post, I want to break it down clearly, without skipping over the intuition or the math.

What is KL Divergence?#

At a high level, KL divergence measures how one probability distribution differs from another. Specifically, it quantifies the amount of information lost when you approximate a true distribution $P$ with another distribution $Q$.

The Formula#

[ D_{KL}(P | Q) = \sum_x P(x) \log \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} ]

In continuous cases:

[ D_{KL}(P | Q) = \int P(x) \log \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} , dx ]

It’s important to note: KL divergence is not symmetric, i.e.,

[ D_{KL}(P | Q) \neq D_{KL}(Q | P) ]

Intuition#

Imagine someone tells you the weather distribution in San Francisco follows $P$, but you believe it follows $Q$. Every time you use $Q$ to make decisions in a $P$-based world, you “pay” a penalty — that’s the KL divergence.

Example#

Say $P = {0.9, 0.1}$ and $Q = {0.5, 0.5}$. You can compute the KL divergence as:

[ D_{KL}(P | Q) = 0.9 \log \frac{0.9}{0.5} + 0.1 \log \frac{0.1}{0.5} ]

Where It’s Used#

  • Variational inference (ELBO)
  • Language modeling (comparing distributions over tokens)
  • Reinforcement learning (policy updates in PPO)
  • GANs (Jensen-Shannon divergence)

Pitfalls#

  • It’s not a distance metric (not symmetric, doesn’t satisfy triangle inequality)
  • KL can be infinite if $Q(x) = 0$ but $P(x) > 0$
  • People confuse $D_{KL}(P | Q)$ with $D_{KL}(Q | P)$ — they’re very different!

Closing Thoughts#

KL divergence is one of those tools that becomes more powerful the more you see it. Next time you encounter it in a paper or algorithm, take a second to think: what assumption is being made about how $P$ and $Q$ relate?
