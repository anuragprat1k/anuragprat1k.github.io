LLM Architectures, FLOP-Matched

The dense backbone from Llama-2 is the de facto standard for modern LLMs, and Mixtral-style sparse MoE is the dominant paradigm at open-weight frontier scale. I wanted to implement both, and their predecessor, GPT-2 and see how the architectural differences between each contribute to a toy task — pretraining on TinyStories at FLOP-matched compute. Architectural progression: GPT-2 to Llama-2 to Mixtral full models Three models side by side. Each shows a transformer block (norm, attention, norm, MLP/MoE) repeated N times, followed by a final norm and a linear LM head. GPT-2 uses LayerNorm, multi-head attention, LayerNorm, and a GELU MLP. Llama-2 replaces all four with RMSNorm, grouped-query attention with RoPE, RMSNorm, and a SwiGLU MLP. Mixtral keeps Llama-2's norm and attention but swaps the MLP for a sparse mixture-of-experts layer. GPT-2 2019 Llama-2 2023 Mixtral 2023 LayerNorm Multi-head attention Full causal, learned pos. Process Rewards: An Overview and Outlook

1. Introduction Enhancing LLMs in complex domains requiring multi-step reasoning, logical consistency, and adherence to procedural constraints remains a critical research challenge. Process Reward Models (PRMs) offer the promise to address some of these limitations by providing fine-grained supervision signals throughout the model’s generation process, both at inference and training.
